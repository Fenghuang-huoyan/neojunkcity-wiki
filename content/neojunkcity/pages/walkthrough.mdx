A walkthrough, for a game that is not out yet, can only point at the sentences Steam has published. This page does that. It does not list item combinations, dialogue choices, or a path through chapter 1. Those were not on the store page or the press kit on 7 November 2026. Copying them from a video would be a spoiler this site is not willing to print as fact.

## Where the full game stood

On 7 November 2026 the full game, AppID **2378230**, was `coming_soon: true` with the date **9 Nov 2026**. The store page said the game was not yet available and that it planned to unlock in about two days. There were no user reviews. `package_groups` was empty, so this page has no price to attach to a “buy this, then do that” step.

The press kit still says the release date is TBD. Use the Steam date as the store’s current plan, and expect the client, once it unlocks, to be newer than either page.

## The only published sequence

Starblade’s store text and the press kit both reduce play to **investigate, drive, and shoot**. That is the closest thing to a walkthrough the official pages give you.

1. **Investigate.** Interaction blocks are context-sensitive. The store says they change as you explore objects and as relationships with citizens change. Looking and using are the point-and-click layer. The pages checked do not name the verbs on the icons.
2. **Drive.** Races are a separate arcade sequence on neon streets. The Highway Authority System is the rule the store bothers to print: too slow is fatal, not just a ticket. No track list is attached.
3. **Shoot.** A showdown asks you to hit threats, spare bystanders, and time the shot. The store does not print a health bar, a damage number, or a boss order.

The press kit says the same trio in one line: “Investigate, drive and blast your way to the answers you seek.” It also says icons multiply the more you interface with objects, which matches the store’s interaction-block bullet. It does not add a puzzle.

## What the first-chapter demo claims

The demo is a separate Steam app, **2474130**, free, dated **7 Feb 2025** in appdetails. The full-game store page calls it the first chapter and says it is live.

The demo description is the most concrete “what you do” paragraph available:

- Explore the **waterline** of the Drowned City.
- Do it as **Buster 09**.
- Investigate, drive, and shoot “through the dregs.”
- Try to find your way **back home**.
- Work on a murder you have no memory of committing.

The full-game page’s demo blurb is a mood line, not a checklist. It tells you to splash in “flesh-eating mite-infested waters,” meet “a giant mutant rat lady,” take “a holographic car” for a spin “in a synthetic universe,” and say hello to “mr.tickles” if you meet him. Those four images are all the page gives. It does not say which comes first, which is optional, or which is a character versus a joke.

<Callout kind="note" title="Demo specs are not full-game specs">
The demo’s minimum is Windows 10, a Core2 Duo E6400-class CPU, 8 GB RAM, a GT 730 or Radeon HD 4830, DirectX 11, and 1 GB. The full game’s minimum is a Core i3-4130 or FX-4300, a GTX 750 Ti or R7 260X with 2 GB VRAM, and 5 GB, on Windows 10 or 11. Finishing the demo does not tell you the full game’s install size.
</Callout>

## What the screenshots are allowed to prove

Six store frames are on the homepage. One shows a dock and floodwater, one a third-person highway, one a shoot-out whose UI reads BUSTER and THE LIZARD, one an alley with uniformed figures, one a car against a skyline, and one a round green room. They show that investigation scenes, a driving view, and a shoot-out view exist in the store’s art.

They are not a walkthrough. The UI name “THE LIZARD” is pixels in a screenshot. The store page never explains that fight, so this site does not turn the name into a boss guide.
